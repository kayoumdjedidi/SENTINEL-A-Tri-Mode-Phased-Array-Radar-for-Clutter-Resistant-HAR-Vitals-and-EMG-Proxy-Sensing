On the learning side, work on continuous radar HAR has steadily moved from compact CNN/RNN back‑ends toward more expressive sequence models. Ullmann et al. treat continuous HAR as a frame‑wise multi‑label problem over spectrogram sequences, using a CNN + Bi‑GRU head to predict overlapping activities and showing that careful handling of temporal overlap and label smoothing is essential once actions are no longer isolated clips [I. Ullmann, R. G. Guendel, N. C. Kruse, F. Fioranelli, and A. Yarovoy, “Radar-Based Continuous Human Activity Recognition with Multi-Label Classification,” 2023 IEEE SENSORS, 2023, doi:10.1109/SENSORS56945.2023.10324957]. Qu et al. instead cast radar HAR as an image‑like task and apply a multidomain fusion Vision Transformer that jointly attends over several time–frequency views, achieving strong accuracy but with tens of millions of parameters and quadratic self‑attention cost [L. Qu, X. Li, T. Yang, and S. Wang, “Radar-Based Continuous Human Activity Recognition Using Multidomain Fusion Vision Transformer,” IEEE Sensors Journal, vol.25, no.6, pp.9946–9956, 2025, doi:10.1109/JSEN.2025.3535673]. Complementary fusion work such as RadSpecFusion shows that attention over multiple radar spectrogram “views” (e.g., different bands or vantage points) can significantly improve robustness in cross‑environment settings, but still relies on comparatively heavy CNN/Transformer backbones [A. Ibrahim, M. Z. Khan, M. Imran, H. Larijani, Q. H. Abbasi, and M. Usman, “RadSpecFusion: Dynamic attention weighting for multi-radar human activity recognition,” Internet of Things, vol.33, 101682, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.iot.2025.101682]. 

Selective state‑space models such as Mamba explicitly target this efficiency gap. Gu and Dao introduce Mamba as a linear‑time sequence model with input‑dependent state updates and a hardware‑aware scan algorithm, matching or exceeding Transformer performance on language, audio, and genomics while using fewer FLOPs on long sequences [A. Gu and T. Dao, “Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces,” arXiv preprint arXiv:2312.00752, 2023, doi:10.48550/arXiv.2312.00752]. Wu et al. adapt this idea to radar micro‑Doppler by proposing RadMamba, which combines a Chan‑DS convolutional front‑end, Doppler‑aligned spectrogram segmentation, and a compact Mamba SSM head; across three datasets (DIAT, CI4R, UoG2020) RadMamba matches or surpasses prior CNN/ViT models while using only 6.7k parameters—about 1/400 of some Transformer baselines [Y. Wu, F. Fioranelli, and C. Gao, “RadMamba: Efficient Human Activity Recognition through Radar-based Micro-Doppler-Oriented Mamba State-Space Model,” arXiv preprint arXiv:2504.12039, 2025, doi:10.48550/arXiv.2504.12039]. For a CN0566‑based HAR system, these results suggest that a scaled‑down RadMamba‑style pipeline—Chan‑DS‑style spectral front‑end, Doppler‑aligned sequence construction, and a tiny Mamba/SSM backbone adapted to beamformed FMCW/CW modes—offers a promising compromise between continuous‑sequence accuracy and edge‑compute constraints.

Beyond assisted living and rehabilitation, radar-based HAR has also been explored for driver monitoring, gesture interfaces, and smart-factory safety, but most of these studies still assume single-subject scenes or controlled backgrounds with limited clutter [I. Ullmann, R. G. Guendel, N. C. Kruse, F. Fioranelli, and A. Yarovoy, “Radar-Based Continuous Human Activity Recognition with Multi-Label Classification,” 2023 IEEE SENSORS, 2023, doi:10.1109/SENSORS56945.2023.10324957; L. Qu, X. Li, T. Yang, and S. Wang, “Radar-Based Continuous Human Activity Recognition Using Multidomain Fusion Vision Transformer,” IEEE Sensors Journal, vol.25, no.6, pp.9946–9956, 2025, doi:10.1109/JSEN.2025.3535673; R. G. Guendel, M. Unterhorst, E. Gambi, F. Fioranelli, and A. Yarovoy, “Continuous human activity recognition for arbitrary directions with distributed radars,” 2021 IEEE Radar Conference (RadarConf21), 2021, doi:10.1109/RadarConf2147009.2021.9454972]. Home and clinic environments are more demanding: multiple occupants, deformable clutter (curtains, plants), and slowly changing layouts all amplify the cross-environment and multi-subject issues highlighted in recent radar HAR work [I. Ullmann et al., 2023 IEEE SENSORS, doi:10.1109/SENSORS56945.2023.10324957; R. G. Guendel et al., RadarConf21, doi:10.1109/RadarConf2147009.2021.9454972]. In addition, long-term monitoring shifts the objective from isolated fall events toward continuous streams of everyday activities and vital-related motion, where model drift, dataset shift, and fairness across different body types become important; existing radar datasets, however, remain dominated by short, lab-based recordings with limited demographic diversity [I. Ullmann et al., 2023 IEEE SENSORS, doi:10.1109/SENSORS56945.2023.10324957; L. Qu et al., IEEE Sensors Journal, 2025, doi:10.1109/JSEN.2025.3535673].

From an evaluation perspective, recent radar HAR papers have moved beyond clip-level accuracy toward metrics defined on continuous timelines. Ullmann et al. report frame-wise and segment-wise performance for multi-label activity streams, emphasizing segmental F1 scores for overlapping actions and highlighting how naive windowing can underestimate fragmentation errors [I. Ullmann, R. G. Guendel, N. C. Kruse, F. Fioranelli, and A. Yarovoy, “Radar-Based Continuous Human Activity Recognition with Multi-Label Classification,” 2023 IEEE SENSORS, 2023, doi:10.1109/SENSORS56945.2023.10324957]. Guendel et al. and RadSpecFusion-style setups likewise rely on ROC–AUC and segmental measures for detection-style tasks such as falls and rare events in continuous streams [R. G. Guendel, M. Unterhorst, E. Gambi, F. Fioranelli, and A. Yarovoy, “Continuous human activity recognition for arbitrary directions with distributed radars,” 2021 IEEE Radar Conference (RadarConf21), 2021, doi:10.1109/RadarConf2147009.2021.9454972; A. Ibrahim, M. Z. Khan, M. Imran, H. Larijani, Q. H. Abbasi, and M. Usman, “RadSpecFusion: Dynamic attention weighting for multi-radar human activity recognition,” Internet of Things, vol.33, 101682, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.iot.2025.101682]. This reflects a broader shift from closed-set classification toward detection and temporal segmentation, where missed onsets and fragmented segments matter; however, most published datasets still rely on carefully scripted routines, short recording durations, and relatively small subject pools [I. Ullmann et al., 2023 IEEE SENSORS, doi:10.1109/SENSORS56945.2023.10324957; L. Qu et al., IEEE Sensors Journal, 2025, doi:10.1109/JSEN.2025.3535673; R. G. Guendel et al., RadarConf21, doi:10.1109/RadarConf2147009.2021.9454972; A. Ibrahim et al., Internet of Things, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.iot.2025.101682].
